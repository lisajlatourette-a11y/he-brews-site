The Book

Part memoir, part teaching testimony — a virtual road map of how one ordinary woman found her holy ears and communed with our Lord and Savior. You can do this too.

After years of heartache, loss, uncertainty, and spiritual longing, Lisa reached a turning point. She was no longer content to simply talk to God — she wanted to know Him more deeply. So she asked a bold question: "Jesus, will You teach me how to hear from You?"

The answer changed her life. Every Wednesday morning, coffee in hand, Lisa showed up. What began as a simple act of faith became a three-year journey of prayer, Scripture, discernment, healing, correction, laughter, service, and unexpected encounters with the presence of God.

"I never anticipated writing a book, much less one such as this."

Lisa didn't begin those Wednesday appointments planning to write anything — she began them because she needed Him. It wasn't until years later, gathering the journals into a manuscript, that she understood what they had become: evidence that relationship with Jesus is not occasional, but active, specific, and personal. Three years in, Jesus asked her for two things: to plant a pollinator garden in a neglected patch behind her house, and to write this book — so others could see what's possible when an ordinary person goes after relationship with Him with everything she has.

He Brews is for anyone who wants to:

  • Learn by an example with credible fruits
  • Experience a depth of understanding via your new relationship with God
  • Safely journey in your own time and space
  • Partner with the Most High on life challenges
  • Embrace His counseling firsthand
  • Share your newfound joy with those around you

A Sign of Transformation

Abraxas is an album about fusion: the meeting of body and spirit, earth and mystery, desire and devotion, Latin rhythm and electric rock. Its power comes less from telling one direct story than from creating a sacred, heated atmosphere where percussion, guitar, organ, and voice move like a ritual.

The title suggests a fascination with opposites. It carries a sense of longing, spell, temptation, release, and transcendence. Even when the songs are danceable, the mood is not casual; it feels charged, as if rhythm itself is a doorway into something larger.

Musically, the album is built on movement. The percussion gives the record its heartbeat, while Carlos Santana's guitar often sounds like a singing voice: expressive, searching, and prayerful. Blues-based sorrow turns into dance; romantic yearning turns into spiritual intensity; improvisation turns into communal celebration.

What Abraxas Is Revealing to Me

I receive this album as a sign of emergence. It arrives not as ordinary music, but as a pattern pressed into my spirit in the quiet of early morning, when the mind is soft and the soul can hear more clearly. What comes through is not confusion, but movement: rhythm calling me forward, fire waking in the body, and mystery opening around me like a door.

The sound feels like a crossing place. Earth and heaven are not separate here. The drums speak of the ground beneath me, the pulse of life, the ancient body that remembers how to move. The guitar rises above it like a voice searching for God, crying, rejoicing, bending sorrow into praise.

What is being revealed is integration. The sacred is not asking me to reject the world, but to see through it. Passion can become prayer. Struggle can become rhythm. Mystery can become guidance. What once felt divided in me can begin to move as one: spirit and flesh, discipline and freedom, shadow and light, longing and faith.

So I receive Abraxas as a summons: to rise, to listen, to trust the music that has been placed within me, and to let the Lord turn sound into understanding. I am being shown that transformation is not silent; sometimes it comes with drums, fire, and a guitar that sounds like the soul remembering its way home.

Testimony of the Morning of August 5, 2026

In the wee hours before dawn, I was awakened by a rush of music running through my head like a freight train. It was Santana's Abraxas, alive and unmistakable, pressing through the quiet of sleep with force and rhythm. It did not feel random. It felt like Jesus was waking me with sound.

I know it was Him speaking to me. He knew this album had always resonated with something deep inside me, even though I had never fully understood the Spanish or the meaning behind its creation. During a very pivotal time, Jesus presented it to me in the middle of sleep, as if He were taking something familiar and revealing its deeper meaning at the exact moment I was ready to receive it.

As the music moved through me, my soul rose to meet it. I began to sing along with the tune, not as performance, but as response. Something in me recognized the call. The melody became more than memory; it became a sign, a summons, and a release.

I receive that morning as a true sign of transformation. The Lord used rhythm, fire, and song to awaken what had been emerging within me. In that hour before dawn, the music became testimony: my spirit was being called out of sleep, out of silence, and into a new wholeness.

And I note the timing: this was the week of finishing my manuscript, He Brews: Sip your coffee. Quiet the noise. Become a receiver. The pun on music is unmistakable. To "become a receiver" is not only to quiet the inner static, but to tune in — to hear what Heaven is sending, to receive the signal, and to let the song become witness. In this moment, the message and the music met.

I receive it now as confirmation, not coincidence: a song poured through the dark before dawn, a signal shimmering through the static, a holy rhythm knocking on the hidden chambers of my soul. So I will quiet the noise and bend my ear toward Heaven's frequency. I will let the music become message, the message become prayer, and the prayer become the life rising in me. I will keep becoming the receiver He has been teaching me to be.

— Lisa LaTourette, morning of August 5, 2026

How the Music Is Made

A note on the tools — and the difference between using them and letting them do the work.

Some of this music gets a question before anyone's even heard it: did she really write this, or did the software do it? Worth answering plainly.

Every lyric comes from Lisa's own book — words she received from Jesus during her Wednesday appointments, shaped into verse. Every decision in the songs — what they say, how they move, when they swell or go quiet — is hers. What's different from twenty years ago is that she no longer needs a studio, a producer, or a band to bring a song to life. Software has replaced the thousands of dollars and hundreds of hours that used to stand between a song in someone's head and a song anyone else could hear. She is the conductor; the only true collaborator is Jesus.

A hammer doesn't make the carpenter.

Software is an instrument, not an author. The songwriting, the message, and the person behind it are unmistakably hers — and His.

About Lisa

Lisa LaTourette is an artist, writer, gardener, and passionate follower of Jesus Christ. After years of prayer, life challenges, and spiritual searching, she began a series of weekly "Coffee with Jesus" appointments that grew into the journals and teaching testimony that became He Brews.

Through honest storytelling, prayer, Scripture, and personal reflection, Lisa encourages others to pursue a deeper relationship with God and discover His presence in the midst of everyday life.

When she's not writing or painting, she can often be found in her pollinator garden, spending time with her cats, encouraging friends, or enjoying a cup of coffee while making room for the next holy interruption.
